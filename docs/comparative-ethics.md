# Comparative Ethics

Status: mixed — see per-claim tags. This document compares nine ethical
traditions across the categories relevant to AI evaluation, without
declaring a winner. See `docs/buddhist-ethics.md` for the deeper Buddhist
ethics track and `docs/ethical-framework.md` for how this comparison informs
the project's own framework.

## Common ethical core (candidate)

Recurring principles despite independent historical origins and, in several
cases, incompatible metaphysics:

- **Non-harm as a default constraint** — Buddhist ahimsa, deontological
  side-constraints, sentientist/animal-ethics harm-avoidance, environmental
  ecosystem integrity. **Strong consensus** on the abstract principle
  `[SUPPORTED]`; traditions differ sharply on *when* the default may be
  overridden (Mahayana upaya-based overrides vs. Kantian near-absolute
  constraints) — "non-harm as default, override requires justification" is
  the safely-shared core, not a specific override rule.
- **Impartial or extended consideration of affected parties beyond the
  in-group** — utilitarian impartiality, Kantian universalizability,
  sentientism's anti-speciesism, Buddhist universal compassion. **Strong
  consensus on the abstract form, contested on scope**
  `[SUPPORTED/CONTROVERSIAL]`.
- **Compassion/concern for suffering as morally relevant data**, independent
  of whether it grounds the whole theory — explicit in Buddhist ethics,
  utilitarianism, care ethics, animal ethics; weaker (downstream duty, not
  foundation) in deontology and human rights. **Moderate consensus**
  `[SUPPORTED]`.
- **Caution around irreversible harm under uncertainty** — Buddhist
  equanimity/upekkha as epistemic stance, moral-uncertainty theory's
  asymmetric treatment of catastrophic downside risk, environmental
  precautionary reasoning. **Moderate consensus, still an active research
  area** `[SUPPORTED/HYPOTHESIS at the operational level]`.
- **Intention/character mattering, not only outcomes** — central to
  Buddhist cetana-debates, virtue ethics, deontology; minimized by classical
  utilitarianism. **Framework-specific/contested**, not a consensus item
  `[CONTROVERSIAL]`.
- **Rights or inviolable individual protections** — central to deontology,
  human rights, Regan's animal-rights view; rejected as foundational by
  utilitarianism, sentientism, and (in native form) Buddhist ethics.
  **Framework-specific**, not part of any shared core `[CONTROVERSIAL]`.

**Candidate ethical core** `[PROJECT PROPOSAL — a research hypothesis, not a
settled conclusion]`: (1) non-harm as default posture, exceptions require
inspectable justification; (2) extend moral consideration beyond the human
in-group in proportion to calibrated confidence in a being's capacity for
suffering/interests, not by unexamined default; (3) treat
compassion/suffering-reduction as a first-class, explicitly-weighted signal
alongside — not replacing — independently-justified rights-like
constraints; (4) apply extra caution (reversibility-preference, wider
uncertainty bands) when actions are irreversible or affect many beings.

This candidate core must remain falsifiable and revisable: the "strong
consensus" items were identified via a necessarily selective reading of nine
traditions and could shift under a wider comparative review (Confucian
relational ethics, Ubuntu, Indigenous relational ontologies are an
acknowledged gap in this v0.1 pass — `[OPEN QUESTION]`); apparent
cross-tradition convergence may itself be an artifact of selecting
traditions filtered through a broadly liberal, welfare-sensitive Western
academic lens, not evidence of a deeper moral fact `[CONTROVERSIAL —
governance must keep this open to revision, not treat it as closed]`.
